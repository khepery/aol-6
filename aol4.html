理由示例（深蓝色）：

The disconnect between [Company Name]'s public statements and internal risk assessments reveals that deregulation created a fundamental principal-agent problem, where short-term profit incentives outweighed prudent risk management considerations. Economic theory suggests that when regulatory constraints are removed, profit-maximizing firms will expand into newly available market segments until marginal revenue equals marginal cost. However, this rational behavior at the firm level created systemic risk when adopted industry-wide. The dramatic increase in [Company Name]'s leverage ratio following capital requirement relaxation illustrates Minsky's Financial Instability Hypothesis, which predicts that periods of prosperity encourage increasingly speculative financing structures that eventually become unsustainable. The expansion of mortgage-backed securities in [Company Name]'s portfolio despite internal warnings demonstrates what economists call "disaster myopia," where the perceived probability of extreme negative events declines as the time since the last occurrence increases. This psychological bias, combined with the moral hazard created by implicit government guarantees for "too-big-to-fail" institutions, provided economic incentives for excessive risk-taking that would not have been possible under stricter regulatory frameworks.

Example Glossary Terms:

词汇表术语示例：

Collateralized Debt Obligation (CDO): A structured financial product that pools various debt assets such as mortgages, bonds, and loans, then repackages them into discrete tranches sold to investors with varying levels of risk and return profiles.

Credit Default Swap (CDS): A financial derivative contract that transfers the credit exposure of fixed income products between parties, functioning as an insurance policy where the buyer makes payments to the seller in exchange for protection against a default event.

Moral Hazard: An economic situation where an entity has an incentive to increase its exposure to risk because it does not bear the full consequences of that risk, often occurring when institutions are considered "too big to fail."
